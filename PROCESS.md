## Tools Used

During the writing process, I mainly used ChatGPT and the reference materials provided by the course. I first watched Dylan Beattie’s talk, *The Art of Code*, and identified several examples that present programming as a creative medium, including Conway’s Game of Life, the Mandelbrot set, and experimental programming languages such as Piet, Chef, and Rockstar. I then used ChatGPT to organise my ideas, develop the essay structure, and connect the examples from the talk to the question of why artists and designers should still learn programming in the age of AI. After completing the Chinese draft, I also used ChatGPT to translate it into English and improve some of its wording. However, ChatGPT mainly assisted with organisation and language. I made the final decisions about the essay’s argument and content.

## One Thing Kept

I kept ChatGPT’s suggestion that code is not only a technical tool but also an expressive medium. The examples presented by Dylan Beattie show that programming does not exist only to solve practical problems or improve efficiency. The rules, limitations, structure, and execution of code can also express the creator’s ideas and aesthetic choices. For example, Conway’s Game of Life uses a small number of simple rules to produce complex and unpredictable patterns, while Piet and Rockstar allow programs to resemble paintings or song lyrics. These examples support my main argument that designers and artists should still understand programming even when AI can generate code. Understanding the medium allows creators to express their ideas more precisely and explore possibilities beyond standard software interfaces or simple prompts.

## One Thing Rejected and Why

In the original essay structure, ChatGPT suggested describing the purpose of learning programming as a way to preserve creative agency. I did not fully accept this idea because “creative agency” felt too abstract and made the argument sound like a defensive attempt to prevent AI from replacing human creators. Instead, I wanted to emphasise that learning programming helps creators realise their ideas as fully as possible. AI can generate code quickly, but natural-language instructions cannot always communicate specific rules, interactions, and creative details accurately. Understanding programming allows me to adjust AI-generated results more precisely and move beyond the fixed options provided by existing software. Therefore, I changed the focus from preserving creative agency to using programming and AI to maximise the realisation of human creativity. In this relationship, human imagination defines the direction and purpose, while programming knowledge and AI help transform the idea into a more complete work.
